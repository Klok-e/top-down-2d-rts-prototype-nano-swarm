# Use one Strategic Controller policy

Nano Swarm has one Strategic Controller policy for Standard and every automatically controlled swarm, including both sides of AI Battle. A well-rounded controller sustains its economy, logistics, support, and fighting force; mounts meaningful attacks; reacts to pressure and lost support; retargets depleted resources; and finishes exposed structures and distant survivors through the same gameplay rules as the player. Public behavioral tests and human play establish that contract without controller rankings. AI Battle remains as symmetric self-play on Standard and Flanks for observing economy, combat, finishing, geographic sensitivity, and regressions without comparing controller policies; the reserved Narrows and Crossroads variants are removed. It runs until Swarm Elimination or explicit interruption, records interrupted runs without inventing an outcome, and has no automatic simulated-time cutoff. AI Battle is an ordinary scenario with layout, seed, real-time pacing, and output-location controls; experiment mode and its controller, side-swap, pacing, and cutoff options are removed. Battle records retain factual effective damage alongside economy, structure, population, and outcome data; lifetime-capped scored damage and Combat Damage Credit are removed with the comparison pipeline. Controller diagnostics retain deterministic review, intent-edit, work-unit, and latest-explanation data while removing wall-clock profiling and its sample history. Deliberate is the sole shipped pacing for Standard, Sandbox, and AI Battle, while tests may inject explicit numeric timing fixtures when their contract does not depend on shipped pacing. Remove the Timed policy, the Adaptive/Timed selection abstraction, pacing selection, and the automated experiment runner because maintaining alternatives and a comparison pipeline encourages policy selection instead of improving one well-rounded opponent. This supersedes only the multi-policy portions of ADR-0022; its novice-opponent objective, shared-rule boundary, and bounded planning requirements remain accepted.

## Consequences

- Owned-intent cleanup remains bounded and eventually visits every owned cell regardless of earlier foreign paint. Public coverage includes more than 256 earlier foreign cells and proves stale controller paint is eventually erased.
- Public behavioral tests are authoritative for stale intent cleanup, resource and support invalidation, shared work-budget enforcement, and terminal planning shutdown. Private tests retain only distinct planning invariants; exact edit-shape coverage is removed after representative fault proof establishes equivalent public protection.
- The Strategic Controller remains one deep module with a small immutable-state-to-owned-intent-edits interface. Plan lifecycle and budgeting, strategy scoring, intent materialization, and private invariant tests may use private internal seams without policy traits, adapters, registries, or selection hooks.
- Active code, interfaces, tests, CLI text, and current documentation use Strategic Controller without an Adaptive policy label or policy-specific constructor.
- Owned intent edits express Paint and Erase as explicit actions rather than a Boolean flag.
- Competitive research remains in an explicitly archived history document and existing ignored artifacts remain historical data. Current verification documentation contains only the sole controller's active behavioral contract and evidence, without comparison commands or promotion gates.
- The surviving controller retains its current strategy heuristics during the cutover. Behavior changes require a concrete failure of the accepted behavioral contract rather than a comparative score.
- Verification fault-proves changed behavioral coverage, runs the repository's full formatting, lint, test, and offscreen screenshot gates, and inspects affected images. Headless observation covers accelerated Standard and Flanks self-play through elimination or an explicit 600-simulated-second interruption plus 120 seconds each of real-time Standard and AI Battle; interruption never supplies a match outcome, and human-tested novice difficulty remains unverified until a human plays it.
