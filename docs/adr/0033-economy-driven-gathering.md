# Economy-driven gathering and throughput-ranked logistics

Workers gathered whenever Gather work existed, because Gather pressure counts unclaimed deposit minerals (tens of thousands) against single units of construction and maintenance. Swarms banked hundreds of minerals in stockpiles while facilities starved and structures decayed. Gathering is now sized by the economy instead of by deposit contents.

- **Gather Quota.** Each swarm assigns `ceil(needed income / gather rate)` Workers to gathering. Needed income is Spending Capacity plus the gap to the Reserve Target, closed over `reserve_seconds`. Spending Capacity counts facilities that are producing or have unmet Population Demand at their full cycle rate, plus Defender charge upkeep. It measures what the swarm could spend, not what supply allows, so a starved swarm still asks for gatherers. The Reserve Target is `reserve_seconds` (default 10) of Spending Capacity, held in Source and Sink Stockpiles; Charger, hopper and cargo minerals are already committed and do not count. [ADR-0043](0043-use-spare-workers-for-gathering.md) amends enforcement to allow Spare Gathering.
- **Gather rate.** The rate is minerals extracted per productive gatherer-second over a trailing 20 s window. `gather_rate_fallback` applies until a full window exists, and again whenever a window measures no extraction or no productive gatherer-time. Gatherer-time, rather than the current count, keeps the rate steady while gatherers join or leave. A gatherer waiting at its deposit for Source room is excluded: counting it would read a full Source as slow Workers and raise the quota when more gatherers cannot help.
- **Bounds.** The quota keeps one gatherer per Gather-painted deposit, so income and rate measurement never stall. It stays within each deposit's work-ring body count, and within one gatherer per full load of Source Stockpile room plus that floor. Without the room cap, gatherers split scarce room into one-mineral loads, the measured rate collapsed, and the quota drove more Workers into the jam.
- **Drop-off beside the deposit.** A gatherer drops its load only at Source Stockpiles within `source_proximity_radius` of its deposit's edge, and the room cap counts only those Sources. Before, it took the nearest Source of its swarm with unclaimed room anywhere, then walked back: gatherers spent 90% of their time walking (median 2,191 units to the drop-off against 710 to the nearest own Source) and brought in 0.25 minerals/s each. When the deposit's Sources are full the gatherer waits, and full-stockpile expansion plans another Source beside the deposit, so Source room grows where gathering is. A loaded gatherer whose Source is lost mid-trip stays within the same area, found from its claim's deposit. Known limitation: a nearby Source with free room that gatherers cannot reach still counts as serving the deposit, so no replacement is planned and its gatherers idle instead of walking to a distant Source. Over standard seeds 11-20 the rate per gatherer rose from 0.25 to 0.33/s, but income stayed at 3.3/s and production within noise (73.7 vs 77.9 bots built per swarm): the quota then sends fewer gatherers, and its deposit, ring and Source-room caps still bind.
- **Enforcement.** Population Demand uses the quota for Gather Workers, replacing per-deposit ring slots. Allocation stops offering Gather once a swarm's gatherers reach the quota, and a deposit stops accepting gatherers once its ring is full. Gatherers leave only by attrition, re-entering allocation after each delivery, so a falling quota never strands cargo.
- **Haul ranking** (amends [ADR-0005](0005-tiered-logistics-flow.md)). Hauler terminal work is ranked by route cost per mineral carried plus `haul_urgency_cost` per urgency level, so a short full refill beats a long trip carrying a few minerals. Charger emergencies and fully aged terminals still form a strict tier ahead of that cost. A facility delivery counts as startable production only when it is the load that lets the next cycle start; loads queued behind a cycle the hopper or incoming deliveries already fund rank as ordinary refill, so a starving facility outranks another facility's queue. A terminal draws only from Sink Stockpiles within two cells of its nearest stocked Sink. Otherwise a far pickup holds the terminal's claim for the whole trip while nearer stock sits unclaimed. A Hauler skips a partial pickup from a Sink that other Haulers are still refilling, unless the delivery is a Charger emergency: new Sink stock was being claimed in 1-4 mineral slivers as it landed, each costing a full round trip. Such a Sink also stops counting as a terminal's nearest stocked Sink, so the terminal can draw full loads from a farther Sink meanwhile.
- **Sink capacity.** Sink Stockpiles have their own capacity (`sink_capacity`, 150), separate from Source Stockpiles (`stockpile_capacity`, 60). Every delivered mineral passes through a Sink, and inbound claims hold Sink room for the whole trip, so a small Sink limited how many Haulers could bring loads in at once. Sources stay small because their room also caps the Gather Quota.
- **Waiting Haulers.** A Production Facility that is producing, or idle with unmet demand, offers a delivery window beyond its hopper: what it consumes during one delivery's lead time, rounded up to whole Hauler loads, and at least one production cycle. The lead time is measured: from a Hauler's claim to its arrival at the facility with the load, averaged over recent deliveries. Time waiting for hopper room after arriving is excluded, so a facility that cannot consume faster does not read as far away. Real claims lasted two to three times the straight-line trip from the nearest Sink, because Haulers start wherever they finished their last job. Before any delivery arrives, the lead time is estimated from the nearest operational stocked same-owner Sink (the straight-line round trip at Hauler speed plus loading a full load). Unloading is left out because a Hauler unloads at the facility's consumption rate, so that time overlaps production. The window is resized on the regional allocation cadence, since only Hauler claims read it. By Little's law that keeps the next load on its way when the hopper empties; a fixed count would leave far facilities starving and near ones holding idle Haulers. The window never shrinks below claims Haulers already hold, so a nearby Sink restocking cannot revoke a delivery in flight. Haulers may claim that space and wait for the hopper to drain, so the next cycle starts on arrival instead of after a fresh trip. They unload only into real free space and re-route if the facility goes idle. With a 5-mineral hopper and 10-mineral loads, a single queued cycle let only one Hauler serve a facility per round trip; two waiting loads, which is what the derived window gives any Sink within 600 units at current balance values, cut starved facility-seconds from 530 to 384 per swarm over seeds 11-14. A 30-mineral hopper alone does about as well (383). Waiting Haulers fix the bottleneck in the logistics rules instead, so they hold for any hopper size; hopper size stays a separate balance choice. Slow Hauler transfer is intended balance. On top of waiting Haulers, the hopper was raised from one cycle to two (5 to 10 minerals), so a Hauler can drop a whole load and leave: +7% bots built and 18% fewer starved facility-seconds on the standard layout, no measurable change on flanks. The wider window also raises Hauler Population Demand, since haul slots follow claimable space, and it is deliberately unbounded: a facility far from its Sinks justifies more Haulers in transit. This is accepted, because waiting loads only help when Haulers exist to carry them, and the measured runs kept 14–31 Defenders per swarm.

- **Reserve stock by delivery time.** A stockpile's minerals count toward Reserve stock in full when a delivery from it to the swarm's nearest Production Facility or Charger fits within `reserve_seconds`, and otherwise by `reserve_seconds` over that delivery's time; stock with no consumer counts for nothing. Distant stockpiles, such as Sources at outposts, then read as a slow supply rather than a ready reserve, without a special case for outposts or for who built them. The Haul Quota's backlog uses the same weighted stock, since far stock can only be delivered slowly however many Haulers are sent.
- **Haul Quota.** Hauler Population Demand is the larger of pickup trips and a Haul Quota, recomputed every 20 s: the mineral flow there is to deliver (income plus stock above the Reserve Target spread over `reserve_seconds`, at most Spending Capacity) divided by minerals terminals consumed per busy-Hauler-second. Aiming at Spending Capacity alone was rejected because supply, not spending, bounds delivery; it asked for about a hundred Haulers. A first version without a guard ran away to hundreds of Haulers and collapsed Defenders below ten, because something other than Hauler count limited delivery, so each added Hauler lowered the measured rate and raised the quota. The quota therefore grows by at most `haul_growth_step` of the current Haulers per window, and only while the last added Haulers raised delivery by `haul_growth_threshold` of their expected share. Haulers count by their average presence over the window, since new ones arrive mid-window and still have to travel, and are expected to deliver at the rate Haulers delivered before they came; otherwise it holds at the current count and retries after `haul_growth_retry_ticks`. Measured against the previous release it changed little: 85.5 vs 73.8 bots built per swarm over standard seeds 11-14 (ranges 56-137 vs 56-92) and 86.0 vs 89.2 over flanks seeds 42-43, with Haulers (24.1 vs 23.4) and Defenders unchanged. Delivery stayed bounded by the Source-to-Sink leg rather than Hauler count, so the guard held the quota near the Hauler count. The quota is kept so Hauler supply follows the economy once that leg improves. The minimum Sink load, Sink capacity, measured delivery lead time and delivery-time-weighted Reserve stock above were then measured together over standard seeds 11-20 (20 swarm-runs each): bots built 77.9 before and 75.2 after, delivered minerals 2.39 vs 2.47/s, all within noise. They were kept because they remove the observed sliver loads and Sink congestion and make the window and reserve follow measured delivery; what limits production lies elsewhere.
- **Maintenance and Build handoff** (amends [ADR-0009](0009-regional-intent-allocation.md)). Each such job holds one Worker, and the first idle Worker anywhere claimed it while Workers beside the structure kept gathering. An idle Worker at least twice as close takes over the job, saving at least four cells of travel, while the holder has not started working.

## Considered Options

- **Measured consumption instead of Spending Capacity.** Rejected: a starved swarm spends little, so its quota would stay low and lock in the starvation.
- **On/off gathering around a stock threshold.** Rejected: it overshoots both ways. A proportional quota with attrition responds smoothly.
- **Releasing gatherers immediately when the quota drops.** Rejected: it needs cargo handling, and the gain is small, since trips are short.
