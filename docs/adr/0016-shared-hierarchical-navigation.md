# Shared hierarchical navigation

All Nanobot Types use shared hierarchical pathfinding over a separate fine grid whose cells fit approximately one nanobot body plus clearance; structure footprints occupy whole cell rectangles matching their visible edges. A chunk connectivity graph includes structures and Resource Deposits and guides detailed routes, accepting modest detours to reduce search work while preserving accurate reachability. This replaces ADR-0008's Hauler-only intent-cell graph; Haulers retain the 0.35 owned/visible Corridor cost sampled beneath fine cells, while other types use ordinary distance.

## Consequences

- Within 144 world units of a destination's work region, a Hauler may take a clear direct approach to an unloading position without waiting for Corridor route optimization. Immediate local progress takes priority over a potentially cheaper Corridor route on this final approach; longer journeys retain Corridor preference. Body clearance, clearing entry barriers, separate working space, and cargo conservation still apply.
- Completed structures and existing deposit objects block every swarm; all work interactions use reachable exterior positions. Movement segments and local steering must respect body clearance, including corners.
- Work assignment uses reliable connectivity information to establish reachable destinations and estimated travel costs to rank them, calculating a detailed route for the selected destination. Occasionally longer trips are acceptable in exchange for fewer searches and more responsive decisions. Estimated cost never establishes reachability: unknown access remains pending, and reservation and cargo-safety requirements still apply.
- A loaded Nanobot retains its valid delivery destination until unloading finishes; a cheaper alternative alone does not trigger reassignment. An invalid destination or reservation, or confirmed loss of access, permits reconsideration. Pending routing and temporary crowding do not establish lost access. Stable delivery commitments preserve reusable routes and avoid unnecessary direction changes.
- Shared per-tick search budgets distinguish pending from unreachable. Active movement receives guaranteed search capacity, while background assignment and construction planning receives a smaller guaranteed share; either can use capacity the other leaves idle. Expensive planning queries must not consume movement's reserved capacity, and sustained traffic must not starve background planning. Clearing and invalidated routes receive priority, with aging for routine requests; affected hierarchy regions refresh when blockers change. The capacity split requires measured validation.
- Obstructions invalidate active routes before entry. While a replacement is pending, a Nanobot continues along the validated safe portion of its existing route toward the same destination, accepting possible detours to avoid unnecessary stops. It stops before the obstruction if no replacement is ready; pending routing does not authorize movement without a valid route. Removing blockers wakes waiting work without requiring valid routes to change. Paint changes affect future legs. Cargo remains physically held during failed or pending routing.
- Normal steering changes speed and direction smoothly through responsive acceleration, braking, and turning. Slightly slower passing is acceptable to reduce snapping and zigzagging; collision safety may require an immediate stop.
- Movement redesign acceptance targets the default scene and crowded economy scenarios: responsive starts, bounded unintended pauses, stable steering, and sustained deliveries. The 5,000-Nanobot scenarios remain measured scaling benchmarks, not a smooth-movement guarantee for this redesign. Limited friendly overlap during congestion recovery remains part of the movement rules.
- Nanobots normally maintain spacing through local avoidance and yielding. Limited temporary overlap between friendly nanobots is permitted during congestion recovery, prioritizing reliable autonomous delivery and work over strict body separation. Completed structures and Resource Deposits remain solid; teleportation is not an escape mechanism. Nanobot occupancy never becomes a global route cost or unreachable result.
- Congestion recovery begins after about one second of simulation time without meaningful travel progress, including jittering in place. Ordinary traffic uses avoidance; stalled traffic recovers automatically and restores normal spacing once clear.
- Friendly overlap is permitted only for transit recovery. Nanobots must occupy separate standing space before building, gathering, or unloading; crowded destinations produce queues rather than permanent working stacks.
- Nanobots circle around occupied approaches to seek reachable free working space on another side of the goal, stopping when they occupy a valid separate work position. This search is part of ordinary approach and precedes treating the destination as full; it does not wait for congestion recovery. Endless orbiting does not count as meaningful travel progress or reset the recovery timer.
- When every reachable work position is occupied, loaded nanobots wait for delivery space while retaining their cargo and reservation; empty nanobots seek other useful work. Waiting nanobots leave approach and departure paths clear.
- Idle and queued friendly nanobots yield into nearby free space for approaching traffic as part of ordinary avoidance, without waiting for the congestion-recovery trigger. Nanobots actively building, gathering, or unloading keep their working positions rather than being displaced by passing traffic.
- When delivery space opens, the longest-waiting eligible hauler has priority over newcomers. Yielding or moving aside preserves its place in the delivery queue so repeated cooperation cannot starve its delivery.
- Each Production Facility holds at most one finished nanobot until a free exterior cell exists, pausing further production until that output is released. Blocked exits constrain throughput rather than creating an internal backlog. Attack obstruction is outside this decision.
- A facility waiting for exit space does not satisfy the busy-capacity condition for Production Pressure. Congestion therefore cannot justify automatic facility expansion; adding production capacity would not resolve blocked exits.
- Destroying a Production Facility destroys any finished nanobot retained inside it, without spawning that output or refunding its consumed minerals. Retained output remains exposed to the facility's destruction until it enters the world through a free exit.
- Finished output waiting for an exit counts toward its type's committed population for production planning, preventing duplicate production to cover that commitment. It becomes available for work only after release into the world.
- When facilities compete for the same free exit cell, the longest-waiting finished output has priority; simultaneous completions use a stable tie-breaker. This prevents repeated preference for one facility from starving another.
- Factorio's [published approach](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-317) abstracts terrain while ignoring entities. Our hierarchy includes structures and deposits because they define the relevant access constraints; chunk sizes and budgets require measured validation.
