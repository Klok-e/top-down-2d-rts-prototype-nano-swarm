All four games contain production, gathering, and meaningful combat. The Standard loss and Flanks-direct late mining plateaus coincide with incoming combat damage and workforce depletion while replacement had continued, rather than demonstrating autonomous self-starvation. For example, Standard Adaptive births rose 44→49→53 between 270/300/330 seconds while population fell 21→13→2 and incoming effective damage rose 3,418→4,686→5,816. In Flanks direct, it had 21 Defenders at 420 seconds but only one Worker and no Defenders by 540 seconds. Cutoff survival in those states does not establish avoidance of a viable finishing opportunity. The report flags no finishing evidence on Flanks or the direct start. Retain that limitation: one natural Standard win plus the exposed-facility, mixed-cleanup, and distant-remnant shared-execution playtests establish finishing capability, not universal finishing success. Adaptive's lifetime cap excluded 167 HP of repeated structure damage across the matrix. That proves repeat damage was not credited again, not intentional farming; capped damage alone cannot rule out farming newly produced enemies.

## Normal-speed observation and runtime cost

Artifacts and the detailed observation report are under `target/ai72/novice-realtime/`. Standard ran for 120 seconds; explicit Adaptive/Timed AI Battle ran for 183.917 simulated seconds with Deliberate pacing and `--realtime`. Both were foreground, offscreen processes controlled through the canonical client, exited 0 after intentional shutdown, removed their sockets, released locks, and removed private runtime/config directories. The AI Battle summary correctly records `interrupted` with null outcome. No services or compositor windows were created.

The real Corridor button returned in 50.5 ms and changed the selected intent, visibly highlighted in `standard/standard-ui-action.png`. Standard's six 20-second waits took 20.087–20.099 wall seconds each. Its opponent grew to 17 Defenders and advanced from about (12,656, 12,546) to centroid (7,266, 7,242); the final image visibly shows traveling opponent Defenders. Both economies and structure work remained active. AI Battle's six 30-second waits took 30.119–30.162 seconds each; Adaptive dealt 3,674 effective HP, gathered 952 minerals, built nine structures, and retained 33 bots at shutdown. The final detailed battle sample had 42 of 51 total bots moving, 13 haul assignments, two build assignments, three maintenance assignments, and no `work_blocked` facts. This supports responsive, useful shared execution without establishing human difficulty.

Normal-speed camera framing initially followed long pursuit midpoints, so the original six images do not prove readable combat. They were inspected and this limitation was not counted as successful combat evidence. A first close-up also caught post-hit travel without a new HP change and was not accepted as fresh-hit evidence. The corrected read-only observer prepositioned the camera and captured immediately on a new hostile HP drop: target health 100→95 between fixed ticks 3,123 and 3,126, attacker cooldown 29 ticks, and target distance 87.94 units. All six fresh-hit images were inspected, with the first and fourth independently inspected again: `combat-fresh-hit/fresh-hit-00.png` shows the bright cyan/white target reaction core at readable zoom, followed by separation and fading toward ordinary movement. Both involved Defenders had 90 HP by tick 3,160, confirming further damage during capture. The paired public states and `fresh-hit.json` retain the exact damage/cooldown evidence. The observation ended cleanly at 52.73 simulated seconds with `interrupted`/null outcome; no gameplay state or AI policy was changed to stage the hit. A preceding helper attempt failed on a null-health observation, shut down canonically, and remains preserved separately rather than counted as successful evidence.

Coarse normal snapshots did not catch an actual recharge event: Standard had a stocked Charger, whereas the battle had low-charge Defenders and empty Chargers at the final sample. Actual normal-match resupply effectiveness remains an observation limit; stocked support alone is not proof of a pulse. Direct shared Charge behavior and charging screenshot checks remain green. This limits claims about resupply quality, not the completed inspection or the shared-rule implementation.

Across 184 one-second battle CSV samples, the median tick-p95 was 4.742 ms, with central 80% range 3.003–7.058 ms; the worst individual tick was 13.431 ms. Median frame-p95 was 16.738 ms, with central 80% range 16.728–16.753 ms. A worst 87.909 ms frame occurred in the first one-second interval before the first screenshot; it is retained as an early startup transient without assigning a narrower cause. Adaptive reviews cost mean/p95/max 0.270/0.392/0.540 ms across all 369 reviews (`p95_window_samples=369`); observation maximum was 0.150 ms. The final partial 55-frame window alone was smoother and is not substituted for the whole-run record. These are measured local costs, not a research-grade promotion gate.

The previously used transient `nano-swarm-ai72-v7b.service` was also rechecked read-only at handoff: `LoadState=not-found`, inactive/dead, `MainPID=0`, and no unit-file path. It finished and was removed as requested; no service was recreated. `scripts/codex` was not changed.
